Part 10 — Putting It All Together: A Complete Offseason Simulation

(New segment. This is a fictional team and roster, built to exercise every rule from Parts 1–9 in the correct order. Every dollar figure below is consistent with the CBA figures used throughout this series — cap $165,000,000 / tax $201,000,000 / first apron $209,000,000 / second apron $222,000,000. Luxury tax bracket math in the final section is illustrative — the full mechanics get their own dedicated segment. No real players, teams, or transactions are depicted.)

June 25–29: The Forced Decisions

Following Segment 5's calendar, three decisions are due before free agency even opens.

Starting Center's player option ($16M). He'll opt in — no decision needed from you, this one's his call, but it locks $16M onto your books either way.

2nd-year Forward's rookie-scale team option. This one isn't due until October 31 (Segment 5, Step 7) — noted for later, not a June decision.

3rd-year Guard: Qualifying Offer. He's a core piece (Segment 1, Question 2) on a cheap rookie-scale salary about to expire. You tender his QO by June 29, 5:00 PM ET, converting him into a restricted free agent and securing your Right of First Refusal (Segment 9).

Building the Real Cap Sheet

With the option exercised and the QO tendered, your team salary picture (Segment 2) now includes cap holds for your two remaining free agents.

Guaranteed salary (Star + Wing + Center option + rookies + minimums):  $95,300,000

           so 300% of his $6.2M prior salary, per Segment 6):          $18,600,000
Cap hold — Veteran Big (Non-Bird, since he's only been here 1 year,
           120% of his $11M prior salary, per Segment 6):              $13,200,000

           so 150% of his $9M prior salary, per Segment 6):            $13,500,000
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Team Salary:                                                          $140,600,000

Salary Cap: $165,000,000 → you are a cap-space team, roughly $24.4M below the cap before any decisions.

The Bird Rights Strategy Call

Per Segment 4's decision logic:

3rd-year Guard  → Core piece → KEEP the hold, use Non-Bird/QO leverage
Bench Wing      → Full Bird, valuable rotation piece → KEEP the hold
Veteran Big     → Replaceable, Non-Bird only → CANDIDATE to renounce
                   if cap space is needed elsewhere

You decide to keep all three holds for now — as Segment 4 emphasized, never give away flexibility before you have to. You have real cap space regardless.

July 1–5: The Moratorium

You identify one external target: a 27-year-old starting-caliber forward available in unrestricted free agency, seeking a deal in the $20M/year range. During the moratorium, you and his agent agree to a 4-year, $84M deal ($20M starting salary, 8%-adjacent raises within cap-room signing rules) in principle. Nothing is official yet (Segment 5, Step 4).

July 6: Signing Period Opens

Following Segment 5's priority order:

1. Handle your own free agents first. You re-sign the Bench Wing using his Full Bird Rights to a 2-year, $19.5M deal (a modest raise, well under his max). His $13.5M hold is replaced by his actual $9.75M first-year salary — this frees up cap room rather than costing you any.

2. Use cap space on the external target. You officially sign the forward: 4 years, $84M, $20M in Year 1.

3. Fill out the roster with what's left. After the forward's signing, recalculate:

Team Salary after Bench Wing re-sign and Forward signing:
$95,300,000 (guaranteed core, minus prior Bench Wing hold)
   − $13,500,000 (remove Bench Wing hold)
   + $9,750,000  (Bench Wing actual Year 1 salary)
   + $20,000,000 (Forward signing)
   + $18,600,000 (3rd-year Guard hold, still active — RFA in progress)
   + $13,200,000 (Veteran Big hold, still active)
────────────────────────────────────────────────
= $143,350,000

You still have roughly $21.6M in cap room relative to the $165M cap — enough that the MLE/BAE distinctions from Segment 7 don't even matter yet; you're still operating with room to spare, and you use a portion of it to add a backup big on a minimum-plus deal.

The Restricted Free Agency Sub-Plot

Your 3rd-year Guard has 2 years of service — just outside the Arenas Provision's 1-or-2-year window... actually, at exactly 2 years, he is still covered by it (Segment 9). A rival team, the fictional Cascade Kings, signs him to an offer sheet structured exactly like Segment 9's example:

Year 1: $15,048,000  (capped at your era's Non-Taxpayer MLE level)
Year 2: $15,800,000  (5% raise)
Year 3: $38,500,000  (jump to "as-if-maxed" number)
Year 4: $40,200,000  (4.5% raise)

The Kings only needed cap room for the average of those four years (~$27.4M) to make the offer. But your matching burden, per the Arenas Provision, is just the Year 1 number — and since you're still comfortably below the cap, matching is trivial. You match within the two-day window (Segment 9, Step 5). He's yours, at those exact terms, principal terms and all.

The Veteran Big Decision

With the Guard now signed, your remaining cap room has shrunk. The Veteran Big's market has cooled — no outside offers materialized. You let his $13.2M Non-Bird hold sit unrenounced (no cost to doing so) and eventually re-sign him using his Non-Bird Rights to a 1-year, $9.9M deal (within the 120%-of-prior-salary Non-Bird ceiling from Segment 3).

October 31: The Rookie Option Deadline

Per Segment 5, Step 7, you exercise the 2nd-year Forward's rookie-scale team option, locking in his cost-controlled Year 3 salary and buying yourself another year before an extension or restricted free agency decision is required on him.

December 15: Trade Eligibility Opens

The Forward you signed in July becomes trade-eligible (Segment 5, Step 8; Segment 8, Part A). You don't move him — but you now have a fully tradable asset if a deadline opportunity appears.

February: The Trade Deadline

By February, your team salary sits at roughly $213,000,000 — comfortably above the first apron ($209M) but below the second ($222M). Applying Segment 8's matching tiers, you're limited to 110% + $100,000 salary matching, not the standard 125%. You still have the ability to aggregate contracts (aggregation is only banned above the second apron), so you package your backup big and a bench guard — combined $14M — to take back a $15.5M rotation piece from a selling team, comfortably inside your 110% ceiling.

Outgoing: $14,000,000 combined
Matching ceiling (first-apron tier): $14,000,000 × 110% + $100,000
                                    = $15,500,000
Incoming: $15,500,000 — exactly at the ceiling. Legal.

Had you already been above the second apron, this trade would have been illegal on two counts: no aggregation allowed at all, and the strict 100% matching ceiling would have capped the incoming salary at $14,000,000 flat.

The Season-End Tax Bill (Illustrative)

Your final team salary lands at $213,000,000 — $12,000,000 above the $201,000,000 tax line. Using the standard (non-repeater) bracket structure referenced throughout this series — roughly $1.00/$1.25/$3.50/$4.75 per dollar across ascending brackets, with each bracket spanning a few million dollars — a $12M overage lands you partway into the third bracket, for an estimated luxury tax bill in the $25–30M range on top of your payroll itself. That's a real, material cost — exactly the "ownership must be willing to pay it" tension from Segment 1's fourth question, and precisely why the full mechanics of this calculation get their own dedicated segment rather than a rough estimate like this one.

Every decision in this simulation traces back to a specific rule from Parts 1 through 9. That's the actual day-to-day experience of running an NBA front office: not memorizing the CBA for its own sake, but recognizing, in real time, which specific rule governs the specific decision in front of you — and, as Segment 1 opened with, never losing sight of the fact that every one of these tools exists to serve exactly three goals: win future games, preserve optionality, and avoid irreversible mistakes.

Where This Leaves You

Two structural topics were referenced constantly across this series but never got their own full treatment: the exact mechanics of the Luxury Tax and the two Aprons (the bracket system briefly previewed above, the repeater tax, and precisely how the tax bill compounds), and the deeper trade-value mechanics around the frozen first-round pick rule for repeat second-apron teams. Whenever you're ready, those would round out the series completely — but with Parts 1 through 10 in hand, you already have what most fans, and a fair number of media analysts, never bother to learn: a working mental model of how an NBA front office actually thinks, deadline by deadline, dollar by dollar.

Glossary (terms used in this segment)

  • Cap-space team — A team operating below the salary cap, able to sign players by simply spending down available room rather than relying on exceptions (see Part 2).
  • First-apron matching tier — The 110%-plus-$100,000 salary-matching ceiling that applies to trades once a team's salary exceeds the first apron (see Part 8).
  • Illustrative tax bracket — A simplified, rounded version of the real luxury-tax bracket system, used here to show the shape of the cost without claiming exact bracket boundaries for a specific season.
